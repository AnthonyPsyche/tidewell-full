Roast level is the biggest lever a roaster has. It changes what the coffee tastes like more than almost anything else after the bean itself.

## Light roast

Roasted for less time and to a lower temperature, light roasts keep the character of the farm: fruit, florals and a lively acidity. They suit pour-over and filter brewing.

## Medium roast

Medium is where sweetness peaks. You get caramel, nut and baked-fruit flavors with a rounded body. It is the easy everyday choice and works in almost any brewer.

## Dark roast

Longer roasting develops deep chocolate, toasted-nut and molasses notes and lowers acidity, with a heavier body. It stands up to milk and suits espresso and French press.

## Which should you buy?

Pick by how you drink it, not by reputation. Brew black and want clarity: start light. Add milk: go medium or dark.
